What's At Stake & What's Next

Win = Round of 16 in Kansas City on Tue Jul 7 — most likely vs the Argentina / Cape Verde winner (probably Messi's Argentina). A first knockout victory either way would be uncharted territory.

Lose = elimination. Two group runners-up, both defense-first, both chasing the same first: one is going home in the round of 32.

Egypt's history run: 92 years after their 1934 debut, the Pharaohs are in the knockouts for the first time ever — and Salah, at 34, is almost certainly playing his last World Cup.

Salah vs his own coach's record: he sits on 68 international goals, one shy of the all-time Egypt record (69) held by his manager, Hossam Hassan. A goal here equals it — if he's fit (he limped off vs Iran).

Australia's young core has arrived: a 20-year-old (Irankunda, with a Bayern buyback) and 23-year-old Jordan Bos (Feyenoord) headline Tony Popovic's generational rebuild — but the Socceroos got here on grit, two clean sheets in three games.

What To Watch For

Salah vs Australia's right sideIf he's fit, this is the game. Egypt's whole attack runs through Salah drifting in off the right; Australia's low block funnels him into Souttar and the right-back. The defining duel — and the biggest unknown after he limped off vs Iran.
Australia's aerial set-pieces vs Egypt's boxThe Socceroos are thin in open play (2 goals in 3 games) but carry a tall, dangerous dead-ball threat — Souttar (6'6"), Burgess, Degenek. In a tight game, set pieces are their cheapest route to a goal.
Irvine & Metcalfe vs Egypt's pivotThe control battle. Australia's St. Pauli engine room hunts second balls and sets the press; Egypt's Ashour–Fathy pivot want to edge it centrally and feed Salah. Whoever owns the middle owns the tempo.
Irankunda's pace vs Egypt's pushed-up full-backsEgypt commit Fatouh and Hany forward; the 20-year-old Irankunda — plus Leckie wide — is Australia's transition weapon into the space they vacate. Their clearest path on the break.
Egypt dead-ball: Salah is the hub — direct free kicks, corners, penalties; his right boot is the delivery threat more than aerial size.
Australia dead-ball: usual taker Craig Goodwin is OUT of the squad — likeliest now Mabil or Irvine (unconfirmed). Souttar is the towering target on every corner.
Set-piece tip: Two cagey, defense-first sides and an Under-2.5 lean — a dead ball may well decide it. Watch Souttar arrive on every Australian corner, and Salah's boot on any Egyptian free kick near the box.
