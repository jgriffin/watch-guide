What's At Stake & What's Next

Win = Round of 16 on Mon Jul 6, most likely vs the USA / Bosnia & Herzegovina winner (to be confirmed) — and a real run on North American soil.

Lose = elimination. A bracket quirk threw two dangerous sides together this early; one of them takes a shock R32 exit.

Belgium's last dance: almost certainly the final World Cup for the golden-generation core — De Bruyne (34), Lukaku (~33), Courtois (34), the aging remnant of the side that finished third in 2018. Coach Rudi Garcia has openly leaned on his "oldies."

Mané's farewell: Senegal's greatest-ever player has said he'll retire from internationals after this tournament — chasing the ghost of Senegal 2002, the only African side besides Cameroon to reach a World Cup quarter-final.

Goalkeeper crisis: Senegal's No.1 Édouard Mendy is out (knee), pushing deputy Mory Diaw into a knockout against De Bruyne's delivery — a genuine swing factor.

French coach, 2002 hero: Rudi Garcia (French) faces Pape Thiaw, a member of Senegal's 2002 squad who assisted the golden goal that beat Sweden in that fairytale run.

What To Watch For

De Bruyne vs Senegal's midfield screenThe single most decisive duel. Garcia plays De Bruyne as a free No.10 and Belgium's whole attack runs through him; Senegal answer with a physical ball-winning trio (I. Gueye, Camara, Diarra). Get a body on him and Belgium go blunt — give him a half-yard and Doku & Trossard run in behind.
Doku vs Senegal's right sideDoku is Belgium's most reliable spark — pure 1v1 acceleration. Senegal's projected right-back, Krépin Diatta, is a converted winger, not a natural defender. If Senegal can't double Doku, this is Belgium's likeliest source of a goal.
Mané & Ismaïla Sarr vs a slow Belgian back lineBelgium are aging and nervous at the back; Senegal's plan is transition — win it in midfield and spring their pace at a back four that can be turned. Courtois is the equalizer, but the moment Belgium over-commit, this is where Senegal hurt them.
Belgian control vs Senegalese chaosThe macro-battle: the more talented passing side that has misfired all tournament, against the quicker, more direct, error-prone one. The game tilts on whether it becomes a controlled Belgian grind or an open, broken-field track meet.
Belgium dead-ball: the edge, and it's De Bruyne-shaped — he takes penalties, free kicks and corners (Lukaku the backup pen), one of the world's best deliverers; with Mendy out, Senegal are softer in the air.
Senegal dead-ball: their best route is their own height — CBs Niakhaté & Seck (and Koulibaly off the bench) are real corner targets. Penalties go to Ismaïla Sarr, not Mané (who stepped down after successive misses).
Set-piece tip: In a tight tie, watch every Belgian dead ball — De Bruyne's boot is their structural advantage — and every Senegal attacking corner, their cheapest route past a slow defense and a stand-in keeper.
