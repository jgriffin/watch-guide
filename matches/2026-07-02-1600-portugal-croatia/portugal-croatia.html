What's At Stake & What's Next

Single-leg knockout, no aggregate. 90 minutes (then extra time and penalties if level). Winner advances to the Round of 16 on Sunday Jul 6; the loser's tournament ends tonight.

The R16 prize: the winner faces the winner of Spain vs Austria (the parallel R32 tie, also Jul 2) — a likely date with Spain looms for whoever comes through.

The farewell subplot: Ronaldo (41) and Modrić (40) are both almost certainly playing their final World Cup. This is plausibly the last time the two share a pitch — a marquee "old masters" billing on every preview.

Portugal's awkward favorite tag: −130 favorites who limped to a 0-0 group-stage finish and only runners-up — exactly the attacking sharpness question Croatia's experience is built to exploit.

What To Watch For

Leão & Conceição vs a patched-up Croatia back linePortugal's pace on the flanks against defenders managing minutes — Gvardiol back from a broken leg, the 37-year-old Perišić filling in at left-back. If Croatia sit deep, the Portuguese wingers get isolation 1v1s; that's the cleanest route to a goal.
Vitinha & João Neves vs Modrić–KovačićThe midfield chess match, and the whole tie. Portugal's young PSG/City-tier pivot press and carry; Croatia's two old masters slow the game to a walk and pick the killer pass. Whoever sets the tempo wins the night.
Bruno Fernandes vs Croatia's deep blockWith Croatia happy to absorb, Bruno's set-piece delivery and through-balls are Portugal's pick-lock. Snuff his service and Ronaldo starves.
Ronaldo (41) vs Modrić (40)Not a direct duel, but the headline. Two of the defining players of their generation, both around 40, in what may be their last act on this stage.
Portugal dead-ball: Bruno Fernandes on corners & free kicks; penalties Bruno / Ronaldo. Aerial threat through Ronaldo & Rúben Dias.
Croatia dead-ball: Modrić is the primary deliverer (his free kick set up the Ghana winner); Perišić the left-foot alternate. Penalties shared historically among Modrić / Kramarić / Perišić (not firmly confirmed). Aerial targets: Budimir, Gvardiol, Pongračić, Perišić.
Set-piece tip: Croatia's most realistic path to a goal is a dead ball. If it's tight near the hour, treat every Croatian corner or wide free kick as a genuine chance — that's how they beat Ghana.
