What Happened & What's Next

France: Through to the Quarter-final (Thu Jul 9, Boston) vs Morocco — who beat Canada 3–0 to get there. Five wins from five, and yet to be truly tested.

Paraguay: Out in the last 16, but the run — topped by knocking out Germany on penalties — was their best World Cup since 2010 and a tournament to remember for Alfaro's side.

The winning goal: Mbappé's 70' penalty, awarded for Diego Gómez's foul on substitute Désiré Doué after a lengthy VAR check. It was Mbappé's 7th of the tournament — level with Messi in the Golden Boot race.

Deschamps' last dance: France's coach steps down after 2026 — this run is his farewell, chasing a second title as manager to add to his 1998 winner's medal as a player.

What Decided It

France's front four vs Paraguay's back-five blockThe defining fight. Paraguay packed the box in a deep 5-4-1 with Enciso alone up top, daring France to break them down — and it held for 70 minutes, France barely troubling Orlando Gill in open play.
Deschamps' bench vs Alfaro's game-managementFrance's real edge was depth. Désiré Doué's introduction on 61' for Barcola changed the game's rhythm and led directly to the penalty — strength off the bench trumping Paraguay's stall-and-counter plan.
Diego Gómez vs Doué — the decisive duelThe moment the block cracked: the young Brighton midfielder's mistimed challenge on the fresh-legged Doué inside the box conceded the penalty that settled the tie.
Almirón & Enciso on the break vs France's controlParaguay's only route was transition through Almirón and Enciso — but France's 76% possession starved them of it. Paraguay managed one shot on target all night.
The moment: 70th minute — Doué goes down under Diego Gómez's challenge, referee Tantashev checks the monitor, and points to the spot. Mbappé sends Gill the wrong way. 1–0, and that was that.

Opta Corner · Fun Facts

  • 1998, all over again. The only prior France–Paraguay World Cup meeting — the 1998 Round of 16 — France also won 1–0, on Laurent Blanc's golden goal, en route to the title. Deschamps captained that side.
  • Five in a row. Beating Paraguay made it five straight wins in a single World Cup — a first for France.
  • Giant-killers. Paraguay reached the last 16 by eliminating four-time champions Germany 4–3 on penalties — only their second finals since 2006.
  • Golden Boot pace. Mbappé's penalty was his 7th goal of the tournament, level with Messi at the top.
  • Two Gómezes, unrelated. Captain Gustavo Gómez (15) and midfielder Diego Gómez (8) both started — and it was Diego's foul that conceded the winner.
  • Total control. France finished with ~76% possession, ~510 passes to ~98, and a 1.45–0.13 xG edge; Paraguay had just one shot on target.
