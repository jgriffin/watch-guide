## What To Watch For
- **Vinícius Jr vs Japan's right side.** The marquee 1v1. Japan defend in a disciplined low block and double-team wide — can they pin Vini without fouling in dangerous areas?
- **Take Kubo vs Brazil's left flank.** Brazil's full-backs push high; Kubo's job is to punish the space Vinícius leaves behind. The two-way transition duel here is the game's hinge.
- **Ueda vs Marquinhos (air + on the shoulder).** Japan won't dominate the ball, so Ueda's hold-up and aerial duels are how rare entries become shots.
- **Casemiro / Bruno Guimarães vs Japan's second balls.** If Brazil's pivot smothers transitions, Japan can't spring its counters. Win that battle and the upset is live.
- **Set-piece / dead-ball tip:** Japan are organized set-piece defenders but carry a genuine *attacking* dead-ball threat — rehearsed corners/free kicks are their best leveller against superior open-play talent. Watch **Raphinha** (if fit) over Brazil's direct free kicks. In a low-block game the first set-piece goal could decide it — and a shootout favors no one (Japan lost the 2022 R16 shootout to Croatia).

## Fun Facts / Quirks
- Brazil are the **only nation to win 5 World Cups** and the **only country to play in every World Cup ever held**. A sixth title is the stated mission.
- **Japan has never reached a World Cup quarterfinal** — the Round of 16 is the ceiling, hit four times (2002, 2010, 2018, 2022). This is the barrier.
- **First-ever World Cup meeting** between the two. And it comes months after **Japan beat Brazil for the first time ever — 3-2 in Tokyo, Oct 2025**, rallying from 2-0 down.
- **Vinícius Jr scored 4 of Brazil's group-stage goals** — the in-form attacker of the bracket.
- **Doan's double déjà vu:** his 2022 equalizers vs Germany and Spain make him Japan's designated knockout momentum-swinger.
- **Ueda was the Eredivisie's top scorer (25 goals)** this season.
- **Moriyasu** is the first Japan coach at **two straight** World Cups and the first to take charge of **100+ games**.
- **Ancelotti** is the **first foreign manager to lead Brazil at a World Cup**, and the most successful club coach in Champions League history (5 titles).
- **Marquinhos captains a 24-year title drought** — Brazil hasn't lifted the trophy since 2002, their longest gap ever.
- **Injury attrition:** Brazil are without teen sensation **Estêvão** (hamstring); Japan lost **Kaoru Mitoma** pre-tournament — two of the most exciting wingers on each side, both absent.
- **Neymar's possible last dance** — squad for what would be his fourth and final World Cup.
