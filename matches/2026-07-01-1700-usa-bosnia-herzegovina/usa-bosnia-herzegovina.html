What's At Stake & What's Next

United States: Win = a Round of 16 trip back to Seattle and the deep home-soil run the Pochettino era was built for. Lose = the host nation out in the first knockout round, a program-defining failure.

Bosnia & Herzegovina: Win = the first World Cup knockout-round victory in their history and a fairytale last chapter for Džeko at 40. Lose = still their best-ever WC run, having already beaten Italy to get here.

Winner's reward: a Round of 16 tie in Seattle (~Jul 5) vs the winner of Belgium vs Senegal, played the same day at Lumen Field.

The carrot for the hosts: first World Cup as host since 1994; Pochettino rotated nine players vs Türkiye specifically to arrive here fresh and off yellows.

Bosnia's history: only their 2nd World Cup ever (debut 2014), and the first time they've ever played past the group stage — historic regardless of result.

What To Watch For

USA's wing pace vs Bosnia's veteran full-backsPulisic, Weah and the US width against an aging back line — 32-year-old Sead Kolašinac at left-back can be exposed for pace. Transition and the wide channels are the likeliest source of US goals.
Edin Džeko (40) vs Richards & ReamThe spine of the match. Bosnia's whole plan runs through their 1.93m captain as a hold-up reference and aerial target. Richards' aggression and Ream's reading vs Džeko's clever back-to-goal movement.
McKennie & Adams vs Tahirović and ŠunjićThe control battle. Whoever owns central tempo decides whether this is a US stroll or a Bosnia rope-a-dope — Adams' ball-winning against every Bosnian attempt to feed Džeko.
USA's front line vs a leaky Bosnia defenseBosnia have conceded in all six World Cup games they've ever played, and in 11 of their last 12 overall. The US group attack (8 goals) meets a defense that historically can't keep clean sheets.
USA dead-ball: Pulisic on direct free kicks and the likely penalty taker; corners feed Richards. The bigger US threat is open play and transition.
Bosnia dead-ball: their clearest route to a goal — almost all of it Džeko's head on corners and wide free kicks. Bajraktarević scored the qualification-clinching penalty vs Italy (taker hierarchy unconfirmed).
Set-piece tip: If Bosnia are hanging in late, watch every corner or wide free kick — a delivery onto Džeko's head is the most realistic way they steal this. For the US, the danger lives in the channels, not the box.
