What's At Stake & What's Next

Egypt: Win = group winners + first knockout berth in their history. Draw = still advances (top two), barring a wild New Zealand swing — the only "unless Belgium win big" caveat decides 1st vs 2nd, not survival. Lose = scoreboard-watch Vancouver.

Iran: Win = essentially through (~5 pts), and tops the group if Belgium don't win. Draw leaves them on 3, likely needing best-third math (8 of 12 thirds advance) plus the NZ–Belgium result. Lose = out.

Played in parallel: New Zealand vs Belgium (Vancouver) kicks off at the same moment — both Group G games are simultaneous.

Fan shortcut: Egypt fans only need Vancouver if Iran are winning here. Iran fans: win and you're in.

Salah vs the record-holder on his own bench: coach Hossam Hassan holds Egypt's all-time scoring record (69); Salah is on 68. A goal tonight equals his manager.

To top the group (Opta): Egypt 61% · Belgium 27% · Iran 11%.

What To Watch For

Salah (central No.10) vs EzatolahiHossam Hassan has moved Salah off the wing into the centre — 1 goal + 2 assists already in the role. Iran's holding midfielder Saeid Ezatolahi screens exactly the pockets Salah now haunts. The game's most dangerous player vs the man tasked with erasing him.
Taremi vs Egypt's reshuffled CBsWith Hamdy Fathy injured, Egypt patch their back line (Yasser Ibrahim / Ramy Rabia). Taremi lives on runs in behind and 1v1 finishing — and is a noted penalty-winner. Egypt must stay disciplined; one lapse is Iran's whole game plan.
Marmoush's pace vs Iran's veteran back lineIran started the oldest XI in World Cup history last time out. Marmoush running in transition at a back three RotoWire flatly calls slow is Egypt's clearest route to a goal — especially late.
Egypt's pivot vs Iran's countersAttia & Lasheen must win second balls and snuff the first counter-pass. Lose the ball cheaply and Iran spring Jahanbakhsh & Mohebi into the space the full-backs vacate — Iran's only real way through.
Egypt dead-ball: Salah is the hub — direct free kicks, corners and penalties; Marmoush and Zizo also deliver. Threat is delivery quality and Salah's right boot more than aerial size.
Iran dead-ball: their most realistic goal source. Corners from Ghoddos/Hosseinzadeh, free kicks from Taremi & Ghoddos, penalties Taremi. A tall, experienced squad that backs itself in the box — and Beiranvand's long throw is a bonus attacking outlet.
Set-piece tip: if it's level near the hour, treat every Iranian corner or wide free kick as a genuine chance — it's the likeliest way this game's deadlock breaks.
