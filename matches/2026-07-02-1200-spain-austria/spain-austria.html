What's At Stake & What's Next

Win = Round of 16 at AT&T Stadium, Arlington TX on Mon Jul 6 — most likely vs the Portugal / Croatia winner, with a possible Spain–Portugal heavyweight tie on the other side of it.

Lose = elimination. Spain are ~73% favourites (-334); a Spain 2-0 is the bookies' base case. Austria's job is to make it ugly and survive to a set piece or a moment.

Austria's 72-year wait: their first World Cup knockout appearance since 1954 (when they finished 3rd) — and only their second WC since 1998. Ralf Rangnick has dragged them back to the big stage in his first World Cup as a head coach.

Spain's depth tax: de la Fuente named zero Real Madrid players — no Morata at all. The European champions still won Group H without conceding, with Oyarzabal as a makeshift No.9 and teenager Yamal the spearhead.

Fitness watch: Spain lost Nico Williams (injury) — Baena starts on the left; Yamal carries a minor knock. For Austria, captain David Alaba and talisman Arnautović are both racing to be fit after knocks vs Algeria.

What To Watch For

Yamal vs Austria's left sideThe 18-year-old cuts in from the right onto his left foot. Austria's makeshift left (Mwene at full-back, Sabitzer asked to track) is the mismatch Spain will hammer — though Yamal is nursing a knock, so watch his minutes.
Spain's possession vs Austria's leaky boxSpain kept ~68% of the ball through the group; Austria have not kept a clean sheet in 12 straight World Cup matches. The question isn't whether Spain create — it's whether Austria can hold out at all.
Rodri & Pedri vs the RB Leipzig pressRangnick's identity is an aggressive press run by his Leipzig spine (Seiwald, X. Schlager, Laimer). Disrupt Rodri's first pass and Austria find chaos; let Rodri and Pedri play through it and Spain control everything.
Arnautović vs Cubarsí & LaporteAustria's veteran focal point (if fit) is largely isolated against one of the tournament's best young centre-backs (Cubarsí, 19). Austria need him to drag a CB out and bring runners on — their only real open-play route.
Spain dead-ball: Merino is the aerial target; Pedri / Baena take the deliveries. Spain's danger is delivery quality more than a towering box.
Austria dead-ball: their clearest scoring route. Real aerial threat — Kalajdžić (6'7"), Gregoritsch, Lienhart, Danso, Alaba — with Sabitzer / Alaba delivery; their group equaliser was a set-piece header.
Set-piece tip: If it's tight near the hour, treat every Austrian corner or wide free kick as a genuine chance — against a Spain side that didn't concede all group stage, a dead ball is realistically Austria's best path to a goal.
