What's At Stake & What's Next

France: Win = Round of 16 vs Paraguay and a clear path — no superpower in the bracket before a likely quarter-final. Imperious in the group (10 scored, 1 conceded, won all three). Anything less than a comfortable win would be a shock.

Sweden: Win = the upset of the round and a golden draw waiting (Paraguay). They scraped in as a best-third team — battered 5-1 by the Netherlands — but two top-club No.9s mean they can hurt anyone on the day.

Winner's reward: Paraguay in the R16, after the Paraguayans stunned Germany on penalties (Germany's first-ever WC shootout loss). No giant looms until a probable quarter-final.

Deschamps' last dance: his final tournament as France coach — chasing a third World Cup (1998 as captain, 2018 as manager). Zidane is the widely-tipped successor.

Heat note for the stands: a ~95°F (35°C) afternoon kickoff at the Meadowlands — sunny, with a ~30% chance of afternoon storms. Hydrate.

What To Watch For

Isak + Gyökeres vs France's center-back trioThe whole tie in one duel. ~£190m of elite Swedish striker firepower against the tournament's stingiest defense (Saliba/Upamecano/Konaté conceded just one group goal). Smother the two No.9s and Sweden has almost no Plan B.
Mbappé vs Sweden's right wing-backMbappé drifts to the left channel onto Sweden's RWB — a pace/1v1 mismatch Potter likely has to double-team, which pulls a body off the strikers. Watch whether Sweden over-commits to stopping him.
Dembélé vs Sweden's left sideThe reigning Ballon d'Or winner, fresh off a hat-trick vs Norway, isolating Sweden's weakest defensive zone. France's in-form match-winner against a patched-up back three.
Tchouaméni & Rabiot vs Sweden's midfieldFrance's double pivot decides whether the Swedish strikers ever get service. Starve the supply line and Isak & Gyökeres become spectators.
France dead-ball: Mbappé on penalties (tied Messi for most pens in WC qualifying); Olise & Cherki on free kicks/corners; aerial targets Upamecano, Konaté, Saliba, Thuram.
Sweden dead-ball: Gyökeres on penalties (scored the March playoff pen vs Ukraine); genuinely dangerous in the air with Gyökeres, Isak and Lindelöf attacking corners and wide free kicks.
Set-piece tip: If it stays tight, treat every Swedish corner or wide free kick as a real chance — with two big strikers plus Lindelöf, the dead ball is Sweden's likeliest (maybe only) route to a goal. In open play, France should dominate.
