What's At Stake & What's Next

Switzerland: Win = a first World Cup quarter-final in 72 years (since 1954, which they hosted) and the breaking of a four-tournament Round-of-16 curse. Yakin's cede-possession-and-counter model has conceded just once all tournament.

Colombia: Win = a first quarter-final since James's 2014 run — and a chance to extend the revival one more round. Unbeaten, and three clean sheets deep.

Played for: the winner meets the winner of Argentina vs Egypt in the quarter-final, Sat Jul 11.

Full-circle Vancouver: James's MLS debut for Minnesota (Mar 2026) was a 6-0 loss away at Vancouver — he returns to the same city for a World Cup knockout.

Coach vs country: Colombia's Néstor Lorenzo is Argentine and played in the 1990 World Cup final — now trying to steer Colombia toward a possible quarter-final against his homeland.

What To Watch For

Luis Díaz vs Switzerland's right sideIn Yakin's 3-4-2-1 the right wing-back (Ndoye, a converted winger) covers huge ground — and Díaz's pace off the left is Colombia's most dangerous 1v1. If the Swiss back three slides to double him, the far post opens for runners; that's exactly how Arias arrived for the Ghana winner.
Xhaka vs Colombia's midfieldSwitzerland deliberately give the ball up and counter, so Xhaka has to control tempo from fewer touches against a Ríos–Lerma–James midfield that dominated possession vs Ghana. Whoever wins the second balls sets the rhythm.
Colombia's clean-sheet back line vs EmboloDavinson Sánchez & Lucumí smothered Ghana to zero shots on target. Embolo is a physical, back-to-goal focal point who scored vs Algeria — can they handle him while also tracking Ndoye's runs in behind?
James's quality vs a compact Swiss blockSwitzerland conceded once in four games and defend in a disciplined mid-block. Colombia's cleanest route through is James's delivery and between-the-lines vision — if he starts (see the lineup note).
Set-piece tip: With two mean defences, a dead ball may be the cheapest goal going. James is the elite deliverer — free kicks, corners, and the likely penalty. Switzerland lean on Xhaka's left foot to aerial targets Akanji & Elvedi. (Neither side has actually scored from a set piece this tournament — a threat, not a proven weapon.)
