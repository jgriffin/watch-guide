What's At Stake & What's Next

Win = Round of 16 at BC Place on Tue Jul 7, vs the Colombia / Ghana winner (that R32 tie is Fri Jul 3).

Lose = elimination. One single leg decides it — no aggregate, no replay.

Petković faces the team he built: Algeria's coach Vladimir Petković managed Switzerland from 2014–2021 — a 2018 World Cup last-16 and the famous Euro 2020 penalty win over France. Now he's in the opposite dugout against his old nation.

72 years for Switzerland: a win would put them in the quarter-finals for the first time since 1954 — when their run ended in a 7-5 loss to Austria, still the highest-scoring game in World Cup history.

Algeria are back: their first World Cup since 2014, dragged here by Riyad Mahrez in what is almost certainly his tournament farewell at 35.

Switzerland the knockout team: beat France (Euro 2020) and reigning champions Italy (Euro 2024) in recent shootouts/knockouts — but have exited in the round of 16 at four of the last five World Cups.

What To Watch For

Ndoye vs Aït-NouriSwitzerland's most direct dribbler against the side Algeria must protect. If Dan Ndoye isolates his man 1v1 he's a chance-creation engine — but Man City's Aït-Nouri wants to bomb forward too, so the space behind him is the duel.
Amoura's pace vs Akanji & the Swiss lineAlgeria's whole plan is springing Mohamed Amoura in behind; Switzerland's compact block exists to deny exactly that, with Akanji's recovery pace the key. Hinges on Amoura's fitness — see the note.
Xhaka vs MahrezThe two captains and creative hubs. Whoever owns the central tempo owns the game — Xhaka dictating from deep against Mahrez finding pockets between the Swiss lines.
Midfield controlXhaka, Freuler & Manzambi want possession and to starve Algeria of the ball; Zerrouki, Boudaoui & Bentaleb want turnovers and transition. The middle third decides whether this is a Swiss grind or an open counter-game.
Switzerland dead-ball: penalties Xhaka (then Embolo, Amdouni); free kicks Vargas / Xhaka / Rieder; corners Vargas, Rieder & Rodríguez (Vargas's delivery is primary but fitness-questionable).
Algeria dead-ball: Mahrez takes the penalties, free kicks and most corners (with Chaïbi) — his delivery is Algeria's most reliable set-piece weapon.
Set-piece tip: In a likely Swiss-controlled grind, treat any Algeria set piece or quick transition as their cheapest route to a goal — and watch the first ball over the top to Amoura or Gouiri.
