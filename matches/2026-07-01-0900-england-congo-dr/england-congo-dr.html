What's At Stake & What's Next

Win = Round of 16 at the Estadio Azteca, Mexico City (~Sat Jul 5), most likely vs the Mexico / Ecuador winner — that R32 tie is played the night before, so the opponent should be known by kickoff.

Lose = elimination. England are −370 favorites; for DR Congo, simply being here is historic — a win would be the nation's first-ever World Cup knockout victory.

Kane on the climb: Harry Kane just passed Gary Lineker (10) to become England's all-time World Cup top scorer with 11. The records keep coming; England are at their 8th straight World Cup.

52 years in the making: DR Congo's only previous World Cup was 1974 — as Zaire, when they lost all three and scored zero. Wissa's goal vs Portugal was the nation's first-ever World Cup goal.

The heritage subplot: Croydon-born Aaron Wan-Bissaka switched from England to DR Congo in Aug 2025 — and lines up against the country he could have represented. Much of the Leopards' squad was born and raised across Europe.

Atlanta's adopted team: the city's large Congolese community has rallied behind the Leopards — a home-from-home in Georgia for the World Cup's feel-good underdog.

What To Watch For

Saka vs DR Congo's back fiveEngland's width and 1v1 quality trying to pick the lock of a compact 3-5-2. With the Leopards sitting deep, Saka is the most reliable man to beat his marker and create from the right — patience vs frustration.
Wan-Bissaka vs RashfordThe heritage duel. Croydon-born Wan-Bissaka — who chose DR Congo over England last year — defends the right flank against England's left-sided pace. His elite, ice-cold 1v1 tackling against Rashford's directness.
Wissa & Bakambu vs Konsa & GuéhiDR Congo's one real route is the counter. Wissa runs in behind, the veteran Bakambu poaches — if England pour numbers forward, snuffing that first transition pass is everything.
Bellingham & Rice vs a packed midfieldThe control battle. England dominate the ball against a deep block of Sadiki, Moutoussamy & Mukau; the game is Bellingham's late runs and Rice's tempo trying to find the gap between the lines.
England dead-ball: a genuine weapon and the highest-percentage route past a five-man defence — Saka & Rice deliver to Kane, Guéhi and the centre-backs (Dan Burn if he features). Kane takes penalties.
DR Congo dead-ball: Mbemba is the aerial target; Kakuta/Bongonda deliver off the bench — a rare avenue to threaten England.
Set-piece tip: Against a deep block, treat every England corner and wide free kick as a real chance — it's the cleanest way through. And watch DR Congo the instant England lose the ball: Wissa's pace on the break is their whole game.
