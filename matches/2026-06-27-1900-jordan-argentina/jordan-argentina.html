What's At Stake & What's Next

Argentina: Already through as Group J winners. Tonight is about minutes and rotation — Scaloni rests Messi (on the bench) and shuffles the XI ahead of the Round of 32, where they meet the Group H runner-up (reported as Cape Verde — to be confirmed).

Jordan: Mathematically out. Playing purely for pride and firsts — a maiden World Cup point (or dream first win) on debut, against the reigning champions' reserves.

The real drama is elsewhere: Algeria vs Austria kicks off simultaneously and decides the runner-up spot. This result barely moves the qualification picture.

Milestone watch: Ali Olwan is one goal shy of Jordan's all-time record (31, Hamza Al-Dardour) — a score tonight ties the national mark.

Messi's last dance: His record sixth and final World Cup. He's scored all five of Argentina's goals and is the outright all-time WC scoring leader (18). Every minute could be his last.

History on the line: Argentina chase back-to-back titles — no nation has defended the World Cup since Brazil in 1962.

What To Watch For

Al-Tamari vs Argentina's rotated flankJordan's one European-level player (Rennes, Ligue 1) is quick and direct. With Argentina shuffling the back line, the full-back in front of him won't be first-choice — this is Jordan's single most likely route to a moment. Watch if he's let run at an unfamiliar defender.
Olwan vs Argentina's reserve centre-backsScorer of Jordan's first-ever WC goal, against a patched-together back line with little rhythm together. His movement and poacher's timing are Jordan's best hope of a famous second goal of the tournament.
Argentina's reserve creators vs Jordan's low blockThe tactical sub-plot: can a second-string Argentina break down the disciplined, compact defence that nearly shocked Asia in 2024? Jordan will sit deep — patience and quality vs organisation and bodies.
The Messi cameo vs a tiring JordanThe night's emotional pivot. A second-half Messi introduction against a debutant nation, with him chasing more World Cup history, is the most likely "moment everyone remembers." Watch the bench.
Argentina dead-ball: With Messi benched, the on-pitch penalty taker is likely Lautaro Martínez or Julián Álvarez; direct free-kicks revert to Messi the instant he's on. Not primarily a set-piece side — their threat is open-play combination.
Jordan dead-ball: A genuine weapon and central to the Asian Cup run. Al-Tamari is the chief delivery man; captain Ehsan Haddad is the aerial target in the box. (Penalty taker unconfirmed with Al-Naimat injured out.)
Set-piece tip: If Jordan win a wide free kick or corner in Argentina's half, watch Al-Tamari's delivery onto Haddad — it's the one phase where the quality gulf narrows against a rotated, less-drilled defensive unit.
