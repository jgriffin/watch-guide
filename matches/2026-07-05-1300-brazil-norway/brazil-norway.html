What's At Stake & What's Next

Brazil: Win and a quarter-final (Jul 11, vs the winner of Mexico–England) awaits. The 5-time champions chase a record 6th title and their first World Cup since 2002 — a 24-year wait, their longest ever. Anything but a win, against a team they've never beaten, would be a national jolt.

Norway: House money with a twist of destiny. Their R32 win over Ivory Coast was their first-ever World Cup knockout victory (they lost their only two prior last-16s, in 1938 and 1998). Beat Brazil and it's a first-ever quarter-final — on their first World Cup appearance in 28 years.

The venue: MetLife Stadium hosts the 2026 World Cup Final on July 19. Sunday's winner plays their last-eight tie two wins from a return to this exact pitch.

Changing of the guard: with Neymar sidelined by a calf injury for most of the tournament, Vinícius Jr has inherited the No.1 role for the Seleção — this is his stage to own.

What To Watch For

Haaland vs Brazil's centre-backsThe defining duel. Norway's whole plan is to get Erling Haaland isolated and into the box on minimal service — 5 goals already this tournament, including the 86th-minute winner that put them through. Brazil's high line is exactly the space he punishes on the break; Marquinhos and Gabriel Magalhães must not be caught square.
Vinícius Jr vs Norway's right sideWith Neymar out, Vini is unambiguously Brazil's talisman (4 group goals). Norway's right-back has to survive him 1v1 without leaving the space Haaland needs on the counter — the game's central tension in a nutshell.
Ødegaard vs Brazil's midfieldNorway's captain and creative hub must find the lanes to spring Haaland and Sørloth. With Paquetá ruled out (hamstring), both engine rooms are reshuffled; whoever controls the middle third decides whether this is open or a low-block grind.
Set-piece box duelsNorway are one of the tallest sides in the tournament — Haaland and Sørloth are both 1.94m, with Ødegaard delivering. Against more technical opponents, dead balls are their clearest route to goal (echoes of Rekdal's 1998 winner, won from a set-piece scramble).
Norway dead-ball: a genuine scoring route — two aerial giants (Haaland, Sørloth) attacking Ødegaard's corners and wide free kicks. Brazil dead-ball: the opposite — their threat is open-play transition and 1v1 quality (Vini, Rayan, Martinelli off the bench), not aerial set-plays. (Designated takers not individually confirmed — likely Ødegaard for Norway.)
Set-piece tip: If it's tight past the hour, watch the two likeliest ways a goal arrives — a Norway corner or wide free kick aimed at Haaland/Sørloth, and a Brazil counter the instant Norway over-commit.
