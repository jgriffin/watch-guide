What It Meant & What's Next

Spain: Won 2-1, into the semi-final vs France — a heavyweight last-four tie, still unbeaten, one step from a final they've never reached as reigning European champions.

Belgium: Eliminated in the quarters. A spirited late push through Lukaku fell one short; Garcia's side ran into a Spain team in full control of the ball.

The clean sheet, finally: Spain had not conceded all tournament (9 scored, 0 against) — Lukaku's late goal was the first ball they let in. A footnote, not a rescue.

Golden generation, last dance: De Bruyne (34), Lukaku (33), Witsel (37) almost certainly bowed out here — the end of an era that never landed the big prize.

1986 flipped: the only prior WC meeting was the 1986 quarter-final, where Belgium knocked Spain out on penalties. Forty years on, Spain returned the favour at the same stage.

The Duels That Decided It

Rodri & Pedri vs De BruyneThe control battle Spain won. La Roja's double-conductor kept the ball off Belgium's orchestrator for long stretches, starving De Bruyne of the half-second he needs — until Belgium's late surge.
Lamine Yamal vs Belgium's left sideThe tournament's marquee teenager cutting in off the right was a constant source of danger and set the tone for Spain's front-foot afternoon.
Jérémy Doku vs Marc CucurellaBelgium's most explosive dribbler against Spain's combative left-back — Belgium's cleanest route to stretch a ball-dominant Spain in transition.
Lukaku (off the bench) vs Spain's young back lineThe late target-man gambit that finally cracked Cubarsí and Laporte — and made the last ten minutes nervier than the scoreline suggested.
How the goal came: Against a side that barely concedes in open play, Belgium's realistic path was always a dead ball — De Bruyne's delivery onto a big target, Lukaku attacking the six-yard box. That's roughly how they got their consolation.
