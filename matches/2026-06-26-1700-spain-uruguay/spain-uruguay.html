What's At Stake & What's Next

Spain win: Guaranteed Group H winners — face Group J runner-up (Argentina) in round of 32. Uruguay eliminated unless Saudi Arabia also beats Cape Verde.

Spain draw: Still likely through as group winner (5 pts), unless Cape Verde beats Saudi Arabia by 5+ goals. Uruguay advance as 2nd (3 pts) if Cape Verde don't win.

Uruguay win: Uruguay top the group (5 pts), Spain almost certainly 2nd (4 pts) — both advance. Group H runner-up faces Argentina in the round of 32.

Uruguay draw: Through in 2nd (3 pts) unless Cape Verde win (also 5 pts). If Cape Verde draw: Uruguay beat them on goals scored (3 vs 2). Tiebreaker is tight.

Uruguay loss: Almost certain elimination — only saved if Saudi Arabia beats Cape Verde and Uruguay's GD holds for best-3rd-place wildcard (very unlikely).

Historical footnote: Spain have never lost to Uruguay in 10 career meetings. A Celeste win today would be their first ever over La Roja.

What To Watch For

Yamal vs Olivera (CB out of position) Mathías Olivera is a left-back at Napoli, forced into a centre-back role today with both Giménez and Araújo absent from the lineup. Lamine Yamal at 18 is arguably the world's best attacker — he'll probe this mismatch relentlessly. If Uruguay's right side of the box creaks, Spain will find it.
Darwin Núñez vs Pau Cubarsí (19) The raw power of Darwin Núñez (strong, fast, increasingly lethal) against Cubarsí, 19, who has been one of the tournament's best defenders. If Núñez can bully or isolate the teenager, Uruguay have a real chance at goal. If Cubarsí holds firm, Spain's composure grows.
Valverde vs Pedri — the midfield soul Federico Valverde is the bulldozer: covering every blade of grass, winning headers, launching counter-attacks. Pedri is the painter: one-touch passing, finding half-spaces, tempo control. The team that wins this duel wins the midfield — and probably the game.
Ugarte vs Spain's build-up Manuel Ugarte (Man United) is one of the best ball-winners in world football. Uruguay's entire defensive structure relies on him winning first contacts and triggering the counter. Spain move the ball quickly through Rodri — if Ugarte can disrupt that first pass, expect chaos.
Set-piece tip: Mikel Merino scored the header that sent Spain to the Euro 2024 semi-finals — he's a real aerial threat at set pieces. Watch Marc Cucurella's deliveries from the left; Spain rehearse these relentlessly. Uruguay's own threat: Darwin Núñez and Mathías Olivera both dangerous at the back post from corners.
Tactical note: Spain press high and dominate possession (avg 68% in this tournament). Uruguay under Bielsa also like to press — so expect an open, intense first 20 minutes before one team blinks. Bielsa's teams are famous for heavy-metal football that runs out of steam late. If it's level at 70 minutes, Spain's superior bench depth (Nico Williams, Dani Olmo, Gavi) tips the balance.
